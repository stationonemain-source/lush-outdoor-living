What’s in the preview

Three things your current site can’t do

Shows how a yard gets built

Eight stages, from the lawn as it is to the first evening by the water, which visitors step through themselves. It explains design-build without a paragraph. At launch it uses photos from one of your real jobs.

Collects the right leads

Plan your yard is a one-minute survey: who’s asking, what they want, what’s there now, timing, budget, photos of the yard, then contact details. You get a ready-made project brief, and builders and realtors sort themselves from homeowners.

Answers questions at 10 PM

Ask the studio is an AI assistant that answers from facts you approve. It never quotes prices or promises dates; it sends people to the survey or your phone. It is capped, so it can’t run up a bill.

Good news

The site is clean

We checked all 19 addresses on the site as a visitor and as Google’s crawler for hidden spam links, the kind hackers plant on older WordPress sites. Nothing found, and an independent malware scan came back clean. Your name, address and phone also match everywhere, which is the part many businesses get wrong.

Website

One screen, 23 words

The whole site is a logo, five black-and-white photos at 253 pixels, and the address. Nothing says what Lush does, where, or how a project runs. Google has 19 addresses for the site and every one serves that same screen. The full-size colour photos are on the server; the site shows them grey and small.

19 sitemap addresses, identical page · no heading, no page descriptions per service, no phone layout
Search

Missing from “custom pool builder Houston”

Google lists Lush for “landscape architect Houston” and “outdoor living design build Houston”, but not in the 20 results for custom pool builder. The listing’s category and the words on the site decide that search, and neither says pool.

Google business category today: General Contractor
Reviews

Five perfect reviews, the newest from June 2025

Every review Lush has is five stars. There are five, over six years. The ten Houston pool and landscape firms Google shows around you hold a median of 232. A steady ask after every build is the fix.

Leads

No way to start a project from the site

No form, no survey, no booking. “Contact” reloads the same page. Someone ready to call has to write an email with no idea what to include, and the studio learns nothing about the yard before the first conversation.

Tracking

Nothing measures who visits

No analytics, so there is no way to know how many homeowners find the site after seeing a finished pool, or how many leave without calling.
